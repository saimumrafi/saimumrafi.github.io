How I think — operating principles

Problem before product

I write the problem statement, the user and the success measure before any feature list. If I can't state who hurts and how much, I'm not ready to build.

Humans stay in control

Especially with AI, good products make people more capable, not more passive. I design approval points, transparency and easy overrides in from day one.

Local context wins

Frameworks from Silicon Valley rarely fit emerging markets unchanged. Payments, trust, language, informal processes and margins all shape what actually works.

Smallest testable bet

I break big visions into the cheapest experiment that could prove me wrong: an interview, a manual pilot, a landing page, before writing production code.

Decide with evidence, move with speed

Perfect information doesn't exist. I set decision rules in advance, then commit, learn and adjust instead of stalling.

Write it down

Clear writing is clear thinking. PRDs, decision logs and one-page strategies keep teams aligned and make my reasoning easy to challenge.

CASE 01

Agentra

An AI-agent operations platform that lets small and mid-sized businesses delegate repetitive back-office work to autonomous agents, while humans stay in control.

RoleFounder, product & strategy
StageConcept & validation
Timeline2026 — Present
DomainAI agents · SaaS · SME ops

The problem

SMEs run on repetitive manual work: invoice entry, follow-ups, order tracking, report compiling, data copy-paste between tools. They can't afford large ops teams or enterprise automation, so owners and staff burn hours on low-value tasks, and mistakes creep in.

Who it's for

  • Primary: owner-operators and operations managers at businesses of roughly 10–200 people
  • Secondary: accountants and agencies serving many small clients
  • Not for (yet): large enterprises with dedicated IT and RPA teams

Core insight

Existing automation tools demand setup effort that SMEs don't have, while fully autonomous AI is too risky to trust with money and customers. The gap is delegation with supervision: agents that do the work, and people who approve what matters.

Solution

  • Role-based agents (e.g. finance, procurement, customer follow-up) configured in plain language
  • Human-in-the-loop approvals for high-risk actions
  • Full activity log: what the agent did, why, and with which data
  • Connectors into the tools businesses already use

Product principles

  • Trust ladder: agents start read-only, then suggest, then act with approval, then act autonomously on low-risk tasks
  • Explainable by default: every action has a readable reason
  • Reversible where possible: undo and audit trails

Business model

Subscription per workspace with usage tiers by number of agents and monthly tasks, plus paid onboarding for custom workflows. Margin is protected by routing simple tasks to cheaper models and reserving stronger models for complex reasoning.

Go-to-market hypothesis

  • Start with one narrow, painful workflow (such as invoice and payment follow-up) rather than a general platform
  • Run manual-assisted pilots with 5–10 design partners
  • Grow through accountants and consultants who bring multiple clients

Key risks & mitigations

  • Trust & errors: approvals, limits and audit logs
  • Data privacy: least-privilege access, clear retention policy
  • Integration cost: prioritise a few high-value connectors
  • Model dependency: keep the architecture model-agnostic

How I'd measure success

Hours of manual work saved per customer per week · percentage of agent actions approved without edits · time to first automated workflow · weekly active workflows · retention at 90 days · net revenue per workspace.

CASE 02

AI Procurement

A concept for AI-powered procurement and supply-chain optimization built for Bangladesh's manufacturing ecosystem.

RoleResearcher & product strategist
StageConcept & research
Timeline2026
DomainSupply chain · B2B · AI

Context

Bangladesh's manufacturing sector, especially export-oriented industries, depends on complex, multi-tier supplier networks. Procurement is often handled through phone calls, spreadsheets and personal relationships, which makes pricing, lead times and supplier reliability hard to compare.

Problem space

  • Fragmented supplier discovery and weak price visibility
  • Delays and stock-outs from poor demand-to-order planning
  • Limited data on supplier performance and compliance
  • Heavy manual effort in quotes, approvals and follow-ups

Concept

  • Supplier directory with performance and compliance signals
  • Automated RFQ and quote comparison
  • Demand and reorder forecasting from historical data
  • Alerts for delay and price-risk

Research approach

  • Map the procurement workflow end to end
  • Interview procurement managers and suppliers
  • Size the market from factory counts and spend per category
  • Identify the single workflow with the highest pain and lowest integration cost

Local constraints I'm designing for

Relationship-driven trust, mixed digital maturity, WhatsApp-first communication, and payment and credit norms. A product that ignores these will not be adopted, however good the AI.

Open questions

  • Will buyers pay, or will suppliers?
  • Marketplace model vs. software-only tool?
  • Which product category gives the cleanest first wedge?

04 — Process

How I take an idea
from zero to one.

Discover

Interview users, observe workflows, study competitors and size the market. Output: a sharp problem statement.

Define

Pick the user, the job to be done and the success metric. Write the PRD and cut scope hard.

Design

Map flows, prototype and test with real users before building. Output: validated wireframes.

Deliver

Ship the smallest useful version with clear priorities, tight feedback loops and a roadmap tied to outcomes.

Drive growth

Measure, learn and iterate. Refine positioning, pricing and go-to-market from real usage data.

05 — Delivery Methodology

Scrum and Kanban,
matched to the work.

The process above is what I do; this is how work flows. I don't treat Agile as a ritual: I pick the method that fits the shape of the work. Scrum when there's a clear goal that benefits from a fixed rhythm, and Kanban when work is continuous, exploratory or unpredictable.

The two systems
SCRUM

Rhythm & commitment

Time-boxed sprints that turn a product goal into a working increment.

My role

Product Owner mindset: I own the product backlog, order it by value and risk, and write user stories with clear acceptance criteria.

Events

  • Sprint planning: agree one sprint goal
  • Daily check-in: blockers, not status theatre
  • Sprint review: demo the increment, collect feedback
  • Retrospective: change one thing next sprint

Artifacts

  • Product backlog (prioritised)
  • Sprint backlog and sprint goal
  • Increment with a written Definition of Done

Best when

The goal is clear, scope can be sliced, and fast feedback loops matter, like building an MVP.

KANBAN

Flow & focus

A visual board that limits work in progress so things actually finish.

Core practices

  • Visualise every piece of work on one board
  • Limit WIP so nothing sits half-done
  • Manage flow and remove bottlenecks
  • Make policies explicit: what "done" means per column

Flow metrics I watch

  • Cycle time (start to done)
  • Lead time (request to done)
  • Throughput per week
  • Aging work items

Best when

Work arrives continuously or can't be estimated up front: research, discovery, learning and support-style tasks.

Why limit WIP

Starting five things feels productive; finishing two is. WIP limits protect depth of thinking, which matters most in research.
